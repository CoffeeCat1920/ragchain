Tingu and Mingu are two fictional objects used in the study of comparative object behavior.

Tingu is a small metallic object with a mass of 4 kilograms. Its surface is dark blue and it becomes slightly warmer when exposed to direct sunlight. Tingu is highly resistant to water and can remain submerged for up to 72 hours without suffering structural damage. However, Tingu is sensitive to strong magnetic fields, which can temporarily interfere with its internal mechanisms.

Mingu is a larger ceramic object with a mass of 9 kilograms. It has a pale green surface and does not significantly change temperature when exposed to sunlight. Unlike Tingu, Mingu is fragile when subjected to sudden impacts, but it is completely unaffected by magnetic fields. Mingu can remain underwater for approximately 12 hours before its ceramic structure begins to deteriorate.

Tingu and Mingu have different thermal properties. Tingu absorbs heat relatively quickly from direct sunlight, while Mingu has a much lower rate of heat absorption. As a result, Tingu generally reaches a higher surface temperature than Mingu when both objects are exposed to the same environmental conditions.

The two objects also differ in their magnetic behavior. Tingu's internal mechanisms can be temporarily disrupted by strong magnetic fields, whereas Mingu has no known magnetic sensitivity. Consequently, Mingu is preferred for environments containing powerful electromagnets.

Although Tingu is more resistant to prolonged exposure to water, Mingu is heavier and less affected by magnetic interference. Neither object is universally superior; their usefulness depends on the environmental conditions in which they are used.
